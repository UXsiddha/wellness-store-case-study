Design
  • Spend no interaction budget on novelty. Jacob’s Law: a marketplace PDP, a standard checkout, so recognition does the work.

03The problem

Supplements, programs, devices, vouchers. No one place to sell any of them.

There was no single place in the app to sell nutra supplements, its own health programs, wellness devices or vouchers.

The wallet made the gap visible. Credit was scoped to appointments and lab tests, so members held a balance with nothing else to spend it on. For supplements, devices and memberships, they went elsewhere.

Primary · B2B2C members

Employees on a Health Prime plan their employer bought. They arrive with preloaded wallet credit and exclusive pricing.

Also · GMC groups

Members covered under Group Medical Coverage, reached through the same plan structure.

  3. TrustLean on patterns members already know, so nothing about the mechanics of buying asks to be learned.
  4. PriceKeep the wallet’s value visible from home through confirmation, so the discount is never a surprise at the end.

04Constraints and complexity

Two constraints shaped everything that followed.

01 · No platform, no commerce patterns
The healthcare design system had no PDP, cart or checkout, and no vocabulary for quick-commerce or traditional e-commerce. Iterated toward commerce, not forked into a second system.
02 · An ASAP go-live
Reuse over custom builds. Vouchers as a fast-follow, not an MVP blocker. Personalization deferred. Designing to what engineering could ship was the job, not a compromise.

06Three key design decisions

Three calls, three consequences.

Decision 01

One store, two buying models.

Problem
Products come from vendor APIs, programs from an internal CMS, and the paths differ in what they ask you to choose.
Rationale
One card system over both. Same shell, two buying models: Add to Cart for products, Explore Plan for programs.
Result
The split shows below the fold. One asks which variant and which pack size; the other asks how long, then lists what that buys.
Value
A glucometer and a 90-day program coexist without the store feeling split.
Sub-flow
Voucher purchase, Phase 1.2: denomination replaces quantity, a structurally different PDP feeding the same cart and checkout.

Decision 02

Unified checkout over a split backend.

What the member sees

  1. One cartOne payable amount over vendor line items
  2. One checkoutWallet, Health Coins and UPI combinable
  3. One orderA unified order ID
The split never surfaces

What the backend does

  1. Several vendorsOne cart holds several vendors
  2. Proportional splitWallet credit split across vendors
  3. Sub-ordersOne per vendor, under the order ID
Problem
One cart holds several vendors, and the backend has to split wallet credit and create a sub-order for each.
Rationale
The member sees one order; the backend splits wallet credit proportionally into per-vendor sub-orders.
Result
A unified order ID over per-vendor sub-orders, with confirmed and delayed states designed in.
Value
The vendor split stays invisible through the entire post-purchase experience.

Decision 03

Wallet-first, everywhere.

The barrier was price: the discount should never be a surprise at the end. So wallet value stays visible end to end, not revealed at checkout.

  1. Discovery · 01

    Balance in the header.

    A persistent header balance, from the first tile. Blinkit leads with delivery minutes; we had none to promise, so the header carries the balance instead.

  2. Discovery · 02

    Member price on the card.

    Member pricing on every card, in the slot Flipkart uses for price and strike-through. Two treatments were drawn: a plain Wallet Applicable flag, and the member price stated outright.

  3. Selection · 03

    Beside the MRP.

    On the product page the member price sits beside the MRP.

  4. Cart and checkout · 04

    Applied against the payable amount.

    Live total recalculation in the cart. At checkout, wallet, Health Coins and UPI combine, and each deduction is itemised at payment.

  5. After purchase · 05

    An account the member can audit.

    Behind them a Store Wallet screen: balance against allocation, total saved, every transaction. Not a discount that appears at the end.

Value
Reframes the visit from “should I spend” to “I have credit to use”.
Also on
Profile, through the My Wellness Wallet entry, and search, where member pricing carries into frequently bought.

Interpretation The wallet is the B2B2C differentiator and the spine of the store’s value proposition. It launched late in Phase 1 and remains early-volume, a strategic bet rather than a proven driver.

10Phase 2In development

When the input cannot be fixed, change what carries the trust.

  1. Observed

    Revenue peaked in April, then softened for three straight months.

  2. Hypothesis

    Inconsistent, low-quality vendor photography was eroding trust at the point of decision.

    The catalog arrived through vendor tie-ups, so the imagery was never the design team’s to control, and chasing vendors for better images was neither fast nor reliable.

  3. Proposed

    Brand Pages: brand-owned banners, curated collections, shop by concern, shop by product type.

  4. Not yet validated

    Ship Phase 2 and measure whether the trust fix reverses the softening.

The end of the journey and the whole point of it.

11What I learned

What this project changed in how I work.

  1. Deconstruct the design system before extending it.

    I had to take the DLS apart component by component to see what could carry commerce and what had to be rebuilt. Extending a system I had not pulled apart first would have been guesswork, and the guesses would have shipped.

  2. Iterate toward what people already know.

    User feedback kept pointing at familiarity rather than novelty. Each round told me where people expected something they had seen before, and the flow got simpler every time I listened to that.

  3. Keep the seams on the inside.

    Four content models, several vendors and a split payment were never the member’s problem. One card system, one cart and one order ID kept the vendor split invisible through the entire post-purchase experience.

  4. Annotate, and hand over the assets with it.

    Annotating every screen and shipping an asset file alongside it did more for delivery than any handover meeting. It made the work easier for me and for the dev team, and it took most of the back and forth out of the build.

  5. Design to what can ship.

    The ASAP go-live made reuse the brief: vouchers as a fast-follow, personalization deferred. Designing to what engineering could ship was the job, not a compromise.

12Next steps

What would settle it.

  1. 01Ship Phase 2 and measure whether the trust fix reverses the softening. That is the direct test of the diagnosis, and the only thing that will settle it.
  2. 02Grow wallet adoption, the clearest lever for the loyalty goal the store was built for, and still the early-volume part of the business.
  3. 03Instrument the design objectives, so future changes are validated rather than reasoned about.
  4. 04Extend into AI-driven personalization, building on the rule-based personalization already live.
